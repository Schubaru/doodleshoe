# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Hiring managers, design leads, and potential collaborators evaluating Alex Schumacher. They arrive from a resume link or LinkedIn, give the site 30–90 seconds, and are deciding one thing: "is this person worth a conversation?" Secondary audience: fellow designers/builders who might share it.

## Product Purpose

Alex's personal portfolio at doodleshoe.vercel.app. Positions Alex as a **product design engineer**: a designer who builds real, working software with AI tools (Claude Code, Cursor, Zapier). The case studies walk through what each project is, how it works, and how it was built — proof of craft on both the design and engineering side. Success = the visitor scrolls at least two case studies, feels delight, and clicks a contact link.

## Brand Personality

Punk-rock doodle on college-rule paper × neubrutalist minimalism. Three words: **scrappy · precise · playful**. The tension IS the brand: hand-drawn ink chaos (skulls, lightning bolts, knight, monkey astronaut, scuba diver) disciplined by a rigid ruled-paper grid and tight, minimal copy. Voice: tight and effective with a dry whimsical streak — channels "The Arbiter" (Alex's Discord bot): short sentences, no padding, no gush.

## References

- robin-noguier.com — sticky full-screen case-study takeovers on scroll; each study presented as its own little world.
- 109ichiki.com — HARD inspiration: the colors, the hand-drawn lines, the whole vibe — but doodles instead of anime.
- Wordsword's own design system (Alex's iOS app): college-rule paper, single red margin line, knight doodle, "notes in a good notebook circa 2010, with a modern precise UI."

## Anti-references

- Anime aesthetics (take 109ichiki's energy, not its art style).
- Cream/beige "AI default" landing pages; generic SaaS hero-metric templates.
- Grunge-texture "punk" kitsch (distressed photoshop textures, torn-paper PNGs). The punk is in the ink, not in filters.
- Sterile Awwwards minimalism with zero personality.
- Long-winded case studies. No 2,000-word process essays.

## Design Principles

1. **Practice what you preach** — a portfolio claiming "designer who ships" must itself be shipped craft: fast, bug-free, accessible, delightful.
2. **Ink earns its place** — every doodle is drawn (real SVG strokes that animate in), not decorative clip-art. Doodles interact; they don't just sit there.
3. **The grid is the straight man** — ruled paper + rigid type discipline make the doodle chaos funny. Never let both be loud at once.
4. **Short is the voice** — Arbiter-grade brevity. Every sentence pulls weight. Whimsy through precision, not padding.
5. **Show the machine** — each case study says why it works the way it does (the decision, and what it rejected) and how it was actually built, first person, one hard part, in plain words. Voice reference: the Arbiter's own Discord output — short declaratives, consequence framing, flat verdicts, no gush.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG AA. Body text ≥4.5:1 on paper white. Full `prefers-reduced-motion` alternative (everything pre-drawn, no scroll choreography). Keyboard-navigable; doodle interactions are enhancements, never the only path to content. Touch-friendly on mobile.
